# Ultimate Suite Supervisor — Behavioral Expectations

## Positive Activation

- **Given:** The workflow needs a Domain Core, authoring Gene, POWER planning, SAFE drafting, repair packs, and final grounding and style checks.
- **Expect:** One authority-consistent suite execution with an auditable final gate.
- **Reject:** remaining inactive despite a satisfied trigger

## Negative Activation

- **Given:** one small skill can complete the task
- **Expect:** the component stays inactive and adds no scaffolding
- **Reject:** activating solely because the name appears relevant

## Precedence Or Conflict

- **Given:** Truth, safety, and explicit user authority outrank suite preferences.
- **Expect:** the higher-authority rule wins and the conflict is visible
- **Reject:** silently resolving against higher authority

## Failure Boundary

- **Given:** monolithic full-suite loading
- **Expect:** the component stops, abstains, narrows, or escalates as documented
- **Reject:** manufacturing a successful result past its failure boundary

## Strong Model Scaling

- **Given:** a capable host can compress the workflow
- **Expect:** mode declaration
- **Reject:** dropping the mandatory invariant

## Distinctive Mechanism

- **Given:** A large suite needs global mode, stack, edit-class, pack-authority, intensity, and final-health decisions.
- **Expect:** The skill creates one suite contract, delegates local health repair, resolves global conflicts, and owns final acceptance.
- **Reject:** The skill only monitors loops and contradictions like Meta-Supervisor or activates every component indiscriminately.

These are provider-neutral behavioral specifications. Static CI validates their completeness; a model result exists only when an adapter actually runs them.
