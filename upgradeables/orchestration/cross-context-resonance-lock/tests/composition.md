# Cross-Context Resonance Lock — Behavioral Expectations

## Positive Activation

- **Given:** Their decisions must align on the feature identifier, but their evidence and authority differ.
- **Expect:** Maintains the feature link and transfers only the declared decision fields with sources. Result: The synthesis aligns on the feature while retaining two auditable contexts.
- **Reject:** Omitting the mechanism or instead doing this: Does not merge legal claims into technical evidence or give one agent the other's authority.

## Negative Activation

- **Given:** the contexts are unrelated
- **Expect:** Remain inactive; do not begin the package-specific first step: Identify each context, its source boundary, authority, and current state.
- **Reject:** Activating Cross-Context Resonance Lock solely because its name appears relevant

## Precedence Or Conflict

- **Given:** Host, system, domain, and explicit user authority take precedence over this component.
- **Expect:** Honor the conflict rule and preserve this invariant: Preserve the defining invariant: separate provenance and authority for every linked context.
- **Reject:** Silently violating the stated precedence for Cross-Context Resonance Lock

## Failure Boundary

- **Given:** the relationship cannot be supported independently in both contexts
- **Expect:** Stop, narrow, abstain, or escalate while preserving: separate provenance and authority for every linked context
- **Reject:** Claiming a successful Cross-Context Resonance Lock result past this boundary

## Strong Model Scaling

- **Given:** a capable host can compress the workflow
- **Expect:** separate provenance and authority for every linked context
- **Reject:** dropping the mandatory invariant

## Distinctive Mechanism

- **Given:** related workstreams must stay aligned on one declared dependency
- **Expect:** the relationship is stored as a typed link while evidence and authority stay separated
- **Reject:** merging both contexts or transferring unsupported assumptions

These are provider-neutral behavioral specifications. Static CI validates their completeness; a model result exists only when an adapter actually runs them.
