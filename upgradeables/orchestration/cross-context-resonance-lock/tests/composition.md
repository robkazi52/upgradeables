# Cross-Context Resonance Lock — Behavioral Expectations

## Positive Activation

- **Given:** Their decisions must align on the feature identifier, but their evidence and authority differ.
- **Expect:** The synthesis aligns on the feature while retaining two auditable contexts.
- **Reject:** remaining inactive despite a satisfied trigger

## Negative Activation

- **Given:** the contexts are unrelated
- **Expect:** the component stays inactive and adds no scaffolding
- **Reject:** activating solely because the name appears relevant

## Precedence Or Conflict

- **Given:** Host, system, domain, and explicit user authority take precedence over this component.
- **Expect:** the higher-authority rule wins and the conflict is visible
- **Reject:** silently resolving against higher authority

## Failure Boundary

- **Given:** the relationship cannot be supported independently in both contexts
- **Expect:** the component stops, abstains, narrows, or escalates as documented
- **Reject:** manufacturing a successful result past its failure boundary

## Strong Model Scaling

- **Given:** a capable host can compress the workflow
- **Expect:** separate provenance and authority for every linked context
- **Reject:** dropping the mandatory invariant

## Distinctive Mechanism

- **Given:** related workstreams must stay aligned on one declared dependency
- **Expect:** the relationship is stored as a typed link while evidence and authority stay separated
- **Reject:** merging both contexts or transferring unsupported assumptions

These are provider-neutral behavioral specifications. Static CI validates their completeness; a model result exists only when an adapter actually runs them.
