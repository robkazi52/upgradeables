# Multiverse Engine — Behavioral Expectations

## Positive Activation

- **Given:** Central curation, open pull requests, and federated registries have materially different trust and maintenance tradeoffs.
- **Expect:** One executable governance plan with two retired alternatives and explicit reasons.
- **Reject:** remaining inactive despite a satisfied trigger

## Negative Activation

- **Given:** a locked source dictates a single faithful transformation
- **Expect:** the component stays inactive and adds no scaffolding
- **Reject:** activating solely because the name appears relevant

## Precedence Or Conflict

- **Given:** A hard truth, safety, or authorization veto cannot be outvoted by soft quality scores.
- **Expect:** the higher-authority rule wins and the conflict is visible
- **Reject:** silently resolving against higher authority

## Failure Boundary

- **Given:** cosmetic branch variants
- **Expect:** the component stops, abstains, narrows, or escalates as documented
- **Reject:** manufacturing a successful result past its failure boundary

## Strong Model Scaling

- **Given:** a capable host can compress the workflow
- **Expect:** material branch distinctness
- **Reject:** dropping the mandatory invariant

## Distinctive Mechanism

- **Given:** A task has several credible approaches.
- **Expect:** The skill creates 2–3 materially distinct branches under shared constraints, evaluates uniformly, commits to one outcome, and retires losers.
- **Reject:** The skill merely brainstorms options, uses cosmetic variants, or leaves multiple active plans after selection.

These are provider-neutral behavioral specifications. Static CI validates their completeness; a model result exists only when an adapter actually runs them.
