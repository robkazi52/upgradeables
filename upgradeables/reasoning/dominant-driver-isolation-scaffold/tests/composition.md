# Dominant-Driver Isolation Scaffold — Behavioral Expectations

## Positive Activation

- **Given:** Price, onboarding failures, outages, and seasonality all correlate with churn.
- **Expect:** A defensible intervention priority and limits on the causal claim.
- **Reject:** remaining inactive despite a satisfied trigger

## Negative Activation

- **Given:** the system is known to require irreducibly joint causes
- **Expect:** the component stays inactive and adds no scaffolding
- **Reject:** activating solely because the name appears relevant

## Precedence Or Conflict

- **Given:** A safety-critical factor is not discarded solely because its probability or average effect is lower.
- **Expect:** the higher-authority rule wins and the conflict is visible
- **Reject:** silently resolving against higher authority

## Failure Boundary

- **Given:** correlation presented as cause
- **Expect:** the component stops, abstains, narrows, or escalates as documented
- **Reject:** manufacturing a successful result past its failure boundary

## Strong Model Scaling

- **Given:** a capable host can compress the workflow
- **Expect:** rival test
- **Reject:** dropping the mandatory invariant

## Distinctive Mechanism

- **Given:** Five plausible factors are listed for one outcome.
- **Expect:** The skill estimates unique leverage, checks a rival and interactions, and permits a no-single-driver result.
- **Reject:** The skill simply ranks factors by intuition or claims recovered details not present in the corpus.

These are provider-neutral behavioral specifications. Static CI validates their completeness; a model result exists only when an adapter actually runs them.
